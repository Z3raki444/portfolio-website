01 / THE PROBLEM

What needed solving?

Raw GTFS schedule feeds describe stops, routes, journeys, stop times and service dates in separate datasets. The challenge is to make those relationships trustworthy and convenient for exploration without pretending that a static schedule is a live train feed.

03 / IMPLEMENTATION

What I built.

01

Ingestion and transformation

Download and parse a long-distance rail GTFS ZIP, clean stop-time and service-date records, and load five related tables: stops, routes, trips, stop_times and service_dates.

02

Checks before analytics

Compare source and database row counts, inspect references and validate expected structure before building analytics models. Offline tests and CI cover part of the pipeline.

03

Models people can explore

Create dbt views for daily station departures, hourly departure patterns and train-service summaries. Streamlit and Plotly expose station rankings, charts and an interactive station map.

04 / ENGINEERING TRADE-OFFS

Scope and limitations.

The system distinguishes scheduled departures from actual events. The data source is a static feed, the DAG is manually triggered, and the dashboard isn't a real-time tracker or a delay-prediction application.

05 / WHAT'S NEXT

Where I'd take it.

  • Deploy a sanitized hosted demo with sample schedule data
